# Research: Professional UI sound-effect libraries for the Laminar launch video

## Summary

For a polished ~58-second SaaS launch film, the strongest verified choices are **BOOM Library Casual UI (Designed edition)** for soft, tactile puffs/clicks/whooshes; **BOOM Library Modern UI** for a broad, coherent digital UI vocabulary; and **Pro Sound Effects Useful Interface** for a smaller, subtler, lower-cost collection with a clearly documented lifetime commercial license. All three reduce design-from-scratch work, but Casual UI most directly matches the requested “soft transition / puff / click / notification” brief.

This report uses only the supplied first-party product and license captures (captured 2026-04-15). Storefront prices are therefore snapshots, region/tax dependent, and should be rechecked at checkout. The source set supports **nine relevant products across three established vendors**, rather than eight separate vendors; products lacking verified official license terms are marked unresolved rather than inferred.

## Findings

### Ranked recommendation

1. **BOOM Library — Casual UI, Designed edition** — Best aesthetic match. BOOM explicitly describes 400 ready-to-use effects spanning clicks, plops, poofs, whooshes, shimmers and grounded modern tones; “drag and drop” use and a coherent palette minimize custom design. Buy the **Designed** edition, not the Construction Kit, unless source-building is desired. The displayed page price is only **“From £105.00”**, so the exact Designed-edition checkout price is not verified. [Product page](https://www.boomlibrary.com/sound-effects/casual-ui/) [BOOM license](https://www.boomlibrary.com/terms-conditions-regular/)
2. **BOOM Library — Modern UI** — Best single-library breadth and strongest backup when Laminar should feel more technical. Its 3,834 effects include confirm/deny, hover, pop-up, select, warning, window and 600 whooshes, with size/priority variants and extensive metadata. The risk is tone: BOOM also emphasizes futuristic, sci-fi and high-tech material, so audition for clean/subtle subsets rather than blockbuster gestures. [Product page](https://www.boomlibrary.com/sound-effects/modern-ui/) [BOOM license](https://www.boomlibrary.com/terms-conditions-regular/)
3. **Pro Sound Effects — Useful Interface** — Best value and clearest low-risk license. It is explicitly described as tasteful, practical, subtle and stylish, provides 753 ready recordings for gestures, clicks, alerts, whooshes and transitions, and costs $99 with a lifetime one-user commercial license. Its only seven files classified as clicks and six as whooshes make it less exhaustive for micro-interactions than the BOOM choices. [Product page](https://www.prosoundeffects.com/libraries/useful-interface) [PSE licensing](https://www.prosoundeffects.com/licensing)

### Perpetual purchase vs subscription/library access

1. **BOOM individual packs:** the displayed à-la-carte purchases can use BOOM’s **buy-out** license, which is perpetual once fully paid. BOOM also advertises Signature Series subscription access. That subscription has a three-year minimum and annual prepayment; productions completed during the active term retain sustained-use rights, but source content must be deleted after expiration and cannot be used in new productions. The supplied pages do not show subscription pricing. [BOOM terms](https://www.boomlibrary.com/terms-conditions-regular/)
2. **PSE Useful Interface:** the $99 pack is expressly a **lifetime one-user license**, not a subscription requirement. PSE separately offers subscription licenses, under which projects made while active remain covered, but downloaded content cannot be used for new projects after cancellation. [PSE licensing](https://www.prosoundeffects.com/licensing)
3. **SoundMorph products:** the pages show one-time digital storefront prices and separate team licensing links, but the unavailable official EULA means this report **does not characterize the license as perpetual or confirm commercial-video rights**. Obtain written clarification before use.

### Practical audition and buy workflow

1. **Spot the picture before buying.** Mark 10–16 high-value cues only: logo/hero reveal, major panel transitions, two or three clicks/thocks, notification ping, metric/result reveal and end card. Avoid sonifying every cursor movement.
2. **Audition the official demos against picture at low level.** Start with Casual UI Designed, Modern UI and Useful Interface. Listen for short tails, soft transients and minimal pitch identity under the actual music/voice mix—not in solo. Reject anything that reads “mobile game,” “spaceship” or “fantasy.”
3. **Make a palette test:** one soft airy transition, one tactile click/thock family, one positive ping, one restrained reveal, each with 2–3 variants. Reuse this vocabulary across the 58 seconds so the launch feels branded rather than library-driven.
4. **Purchase one primary pack first.** Recommended default: **Casual UI Designed** after verifying its exact checkout price. If its demo is too playful, buy **Useful Interface**; if Laminar needs a more technical tone and future reuse justifies the cost, buy **Modern UI**.
5. **Add motion only if the rough cut exposes a gap.** Consider SoundMorph Motion Graphics for transitions only after SoundMorph supplies/restores binding license terms. Otherwise Modern UI already provides 600 whooshes and avoids a second license regime.
6. **License the actual user/entity.** BOOM and PSE standard terms are one-user; if multiple Laminar staff or an external editor will access source WAVs, obtain the appropriate multi-user license rather than sharing files. Deliver the mixed video/audio master—not the library WAV folder—to unlicensed collaborators.
7. **Keep evidence.** Save invoice, the license version/date, product metadata/content list and any written SoundMorph clarification with the project archive. Do not redistribute raw WAVs or clean isolated library stems.

## Gaps

- **SoundMorph licensing is the material unresolved issue.** Its official EULA link reportedly returned 404 at capture time; no conclusions about commercial synchronization, perpetuity or source-WAV redistribution should be drawn from product-page checkout language alone. Ask SoundMorph support for the binding single-user EULA and retain the response.
- BOOM’s “From £105” pages do not reveal the exact Designed-edition price in the captures. Confirm Casual UI Designed and Magic UI Designed pricing at checkout.
- The source set covers nine credible products but only three vendors. A broader vendor comparison would require fresh first-party captures for Soundly, Sonniss, A Sound Effect, Adobe Stock, Artlist and Epidemic Sound.
- Aesthetic ranking is based on first-party descriptions/specifications, not a synchronized listening test against the Laminar cut. The final choice should follow the audition workflow above.
