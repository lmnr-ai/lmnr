# Ultimate 3 — authored sound-design principles

## Recommendation and evidence boundary

**Build one small sonic world: soft material contact, precise digital articulation, and deliberate silence. Score changes in meaning—not every moving pixel.** Professional polish should come from selection, phrasing, material coherence, and mix perspective, not a larger library of clicks.

This is research and a proposed production brief for a **separate Ultimate 3 sound pass**, replacing all existing SFX with original synthesis or assets whose production use is properly licensed. It is not an implementation, audio render, or listening report. No repository files were modified; no audio was uploaded, generated, or auditioned.

## 2. Authored motion is not interactive UI feedback

**Interactive UI:** a person causes an action; feedback must be intelligible, timely, repeatable, and compatible with system controls and other audio. Event timing is largely unknown in advance. Repetition and interruption behavior matter greatly. Apple’s relevant advice belongs to this context. [S8]

**Authored motion film:** the editor controls the sequence and can decide where attention moves, what is anticipated, and what remains silent. A visible cursor, send action, count, or spinner is part of an explanation—not necessarily a live input needing confirmation. The film can use lead-ins, musical phrases, off-screen continuity, and release after an action. Antfood’s story/world-led process is the closer model. [S1–S3]

**Proposal:** classify each event as **narrative**, **material**, **supporting**, or **silent** before selecting a sound. Represent a count as one evolving gesture, a cluster flight as a collective movement, and typing as a thought or phrase—not a keystroke simulator. Replace “everything has audio” with “the intended reading order is audible.”

The film should still make sense muted. That is a design criterion, not permission to add subtitles or other visuals contrary to the approved plan.

## 3. Sonic grammar: soft tactile + crisp digital

These are proposed art-direction rules, not verified descriptions of the existing film audio.

### Material and role vocabulary

- **Soft tactile:** compressed felt, fine paper, muted polymer, or satin-like friction as *metaphors*. Rounded excitation; audible but short body; little brittle top end. Avoid an assortment of literal wood, glass, metal, and rubber sounds unless their differences mean something.
- **Crisp digital:** clearly timed, spectrally economical, pitch-stable gestures. Precision should come from a legible onset and clean ending—not an ultrasonic tick, square-wave edge, or loud notification ping.
- **Shared signature:** a rounded body with a brief, quieter bright articulation. Keep one contour and related decay behavior across families. A travel gesture can expose the friction component; a settlement can expose the body; a reveal can expose the tone.
- **Shared acoustic space:** close and mostly dry, with a small, dark room impression. Widen major transitions selectively; keep important attacks center-compatible. No mandatory reverb on everything.
- **Pitch discipline:** if effects sustain enough to imply notes, choose their pitches against the actual music after audition. Do not inherit keys or modes from old soundtrack studies automatically. Inharmonic material resonances need not be forced into a scale, but must not ring distractingly against it.

### Attention hierarchy

1. **Meaning / voiceover, if present:** intelligibility and reading time have priority.
2. **One chosen narrative foreground:** a reveal, warning focus, activation, or resolution—not all of them at once.
3. **Material movement:** supports direction and mass without demanding attention.
4. **Micro-detail:** mostly omitted; retained only when it clarifies character or causality.

Music can own emotion while SFX own material and select landmarks. At a hero moment, hand attention between them instead of increasing both. A warning may be narratively important without being the loudest sound.

**Editorial starting constraint:** allow only one dominant SFX gesture at a time. If two gestures collide, merge their roles or remove the weaker one before reaching for compression. This is a working constraint, not a universal production rule.

## 5. Original synthesis recipes to prototype later

**All numbers in this section are proposed experiment ranges, not values prescribed by the sources and not a validated preset. No sounds have been generated or heard.** Use them to create two or three controlled alternatives, then retain only what earns its place in the actual mix.

The conceptual foundation is excitation → resonant body → time-varying damping, with separate transient and body behavior. [S4–S5] A small bank of damped sine modes or resonant filters can suggest material without claiming a physically accurate model. Normalize the summed excitation/modes and monitor intermediate levels; adding resonators can create unexpectedly large peaks.

### A. Muted contact / settlement

**Job:** a panel comes to rest, a cluster becomes organized, a surface finds its position.

- **Excitation:** short seeded noise burst, initially **4–12 ms**, shaped with a smooth rise rather than a one-sample impulse. Low-pass the excitation around **2–5 kHz** before it reaches the body.
- **Resonances:** try three damped modes around **350–650 Hz**, then approximately **1.6× and 2.3×** the first mode. These are invented material-color ratios, not measured properties of felt or polymer. Keep upper modes quieter and shorter than the lowest.
- **Envelopes / filtering:** body decay **70–160 ms**; upper-mode decay **20–70 ms**. Apply a final fade to zero rather than abruptly stopping the oscillator. Remove unnecessary low-frequency energy only as needed in the mix.
- **Variation:** a few fixed alternatives with slight excitation-color and decay changes, roughly **±5–10%**. Keep the family’s identity and semantic importance stable; do not randomize its apparent mass every time.
- **Space:** center or gently position with the object; dry first, tiny dark-room send second. It should read as contact, not a hollow knock or a kick drum.

### B. Digital articulation / clarity motif

**Job:** a meaningful state becomes clear; a selected activation or send resolves.

- **Excitation / body:** a sine carrier with a quieter second partial, or restrained two-operator FM with modulator:carrier ratio **2:1**. Try FM index **0.2–0.8** at the onset, decaying toward zero over **20–60 ms** so brightness resolves into a simpler tone.
- **Envelope:** smooth attack **3–8 ms**, decay **90–220 ms**, no sustained notification-like plateau; fade the end. Audition a starting carrier region of **600–1200 Hz**, then choose exact pitch/register from the actual music—not from this range alone.
- **Filtering:** omit broadband click by default. If intelligibility needs more articulation, add a very quiet, band-limited version of A’s excitation; do not simply add a loud sharp tick.
- **Variation:** preserve the motif’s interval/contour and timing. Vary brightness or body length slightly rather than selecting random scale notes. Test a single tone before proposing a two-note identity.
- **Space:** narrow dry attack with a slightly wider, quieter tail if needed. The cue must remain clear in mono and must not resemble a familiar system alert.

### C. Satin travel / chapter-slide gesture

**Job:** communicate the continuous Cost → Flow displacement, or another selected large movement.

- **Excitation:** continuous pink-like/filtered noise; no impact required. Build a smooth amplitude curve from the actual movement’s speed, then art-direct it so small speed fluctuations do not become audible chatter.
- **Resonance / filtering:** a broad band-pass moving within about **700 Hz–3 kHz**, moderate bandwidth and low resonance. Avoid a narrow whistling sweep. Filter trajectory need not follow literal screen Y or always sweep upward in pitch.
- **Envelope:** for the default 1.2-second entry, span the movement with softened **40–100 ms** opening/closing edges. Put its energy near the meaningful acceleration, then leave room for the incoming title. Optional arrival A should be quieter than the travel if the picture has no hard impact.
- **Variation:** make separate short/long versions from edited motion duration; do not blindly time-stretch a rendered whoosh and change its timbre.
- **Space:** subtle breadth, mono-compatible center energy, no hard left/right flyby for vertical travel. Do not use a sub-drop or blockbuster riser to make the panel seem important.

### D. Collective particles / clusters

**Job:** many warnings or elements become an organized whole.

- **Excitation:** grains derived from the same filtered-noise/muted-contact palette, **15–45 ms** long, with smooth short envelopes. Schedule a handful of audible grouped gestures, not one grain per visible object.
- **Resonance / filtering:** mostly damped, weakly pitched bodies; gradually reduce spectral spread as the visual organization becomes stable. Avoid random bright notes that suggest a game reward sequence.
- **Envelope / variation:** begin with a modest density, shape it toward convergence, then end with a single A-family settlement. Use a fixed seed and curated alternates; the export must not reroll the rhythm.
- **Space:** begin slightly distributed, gather toward a center-compatible image. Do not collapse width so abruptly that it sounds like a phase effect. Test isolated particles only after checking the combined texture for spray-can hiss or machine-gun repetition.

### E. Warning focus without alarm

**Job:** support the authored warning-centered zoom or a meaningful problem state—not simulate a real system emergency.

- **Excitation / body:** one muted contact plus a short, darker tonal body, initially **300–700 Hz**; smooth attack **8–20 ms**, decay **120–250 ms**.
- **Resonance / filtering:** try a lower or more damped version of the shared motif. Avoid a beating detuned pair, repeated alternating tones, siren glissando, and harsh sawtooth buzz.
- **Variation:** normally one authored version per semantic role; no random urgency. If every warning cue feels anxious, remove it and let the picture carry the warning.
- **Space:** close, centered, little or no reverb. Quiet emphasis plus a brief reduction of competing texture can focus attention more tastefully than a louder alert.

### F. Resolved identity / static logo

**Job:** make the ending feel intentional without inventing visual action.

- Reuse B’s established spectral fingerprint, optionally with A’s rounded body; do **not** introduce a new stock logo chime.
- Try a smooth **8–20 ms** attack with **250–600 ms** decay; select any interval only after auditioning the music. Let the brighter mode finish first.
- Keep the tail short enough to resolve within the plan’s one-second logo hold and the **62-second** export. Audition music-only as a serious alternative.
- Use the common room rather than a suddenly enormous hall; identity should come from recognition and placement, not louder mastering.

### Shared transient and spatial discipline

Use finite gain ramps at starts/stops and parameter changes. Avoid oscillator discontinuities, abrupt filter jumps, excessive resonant gain, and unintended DC. Reduce harshness at the excitation or mode envelope before relying on a master limiter. A limiter cannot turn an unpleasant attack into good material design.

Initial common-room experiment: algorithmic short room, **0.15–0.45 s** decay, high-frequency damping around **3–6 kHz**, wet contribution roughly **18–26 dB below dry**. These are comparison settings, not a required mix. Essential attacks stay mono-compatible; avoid phase-inverted widening and casually duplicated delay channels. If using convolution later, the impulse response also needs provenance.

## 6. Synchronization, anticipation, rhythm, and negative space

**Proposal: distinguish three timing anchors.** A sound has an onset, a perceptual accent, and a tail. The file’s first sample is not necessarily the perceived hit. Mark the visual meaning first: movement begins, contact occurs, information becomes readable, or a state resolves.

- **Sync:** place contact/settlement accents on the visible arrival, not indiscriminately on the beginning of an easing curve. At 30 fps, one frame is about 33.3 ms; align against exported frames and audition the perceived accent rather than declaring sample accuracy sufficient.
- **Anticipation:** a soft breath before a large continuous transition can prepare attention. Compare **0, 1, or 2 frames** of lead as an editorial experiment. Keep the arrival anchor stable. Avoid pre-triggering literal send confirmations or warning outcomes so they seem to occur before their causes.
- **Release:** tails may connect chapters, but a bridge must not imply a visual transition the plan does not contain. The direct chapter-1/2 boundary does not automatically need punctuation.
- **Rhythm:** let selected gestures form phrases; let music supply pulse when appropriate. Do not quantize hard picture contacts away from their frames to chase a beat. Use anticipation, duration, or arrangement changes to reconcile picture and music, subject to approval.
- **Negative space:** intentionally omit SFX under stationary clouds, frozen budget, reading holds, and unresolved placeholder cards. “No SFX” can still mean music continues; distinguish it from complete silence. Delete redundant cues until their absence damages comprehension or character, then restore only those.
- **Contrast:** vary density, register, dryness, and duration before increasing peak level. If everything sparkles, nothing is crisp; if everything whooshes, nothing has mass.

**Proposed film-scale arc:** establish the signature in Ultimate 2; constrain texture through Cost; open the sound field at Flow; gather and resolve through Issue clusters; release into the logo. The most important reveal should get the clearest space, not automatically the largest sound. This applies Ableton’s arc and subtractive thinking [S6–S7], but the exact climax, phrase lengths, and musical cadence remain **pending actual music and picture audition**. Do not infer a BPM from the filename or invent a tonal map.

## 7. Mix music, SFX, and any voice as one arrangement

No voiceover presence is assumed. If voice exists, test its real recording; do not design around an imagined voice register.

1. **Choose ownership before EQ.** At each moment identify what must be understood. Remove, move, shorten, or darken a competing sound before turning the foreground up. A continuous airy whoosh can mask speech or music detail even if it sounds gentle alone.
2. **Work in context, then solo to diagnose.** Music-only and SFX-only passes reveal problems, but approve the combined result. Soloed SFX need not sound impressive.
3. **Separate by time and envelope first.** Avoid stacking a sharp music attack, a digital ping, and a material impact on every beat. Let a rounded body occupy a gap after a music transient, or omit its bright component.
4. **Then address frequency competition.** Inspect the actual overlapping region. FabFilter’s collision display is explicitly an indication, not a verdict. [S9] Try a small, localized reduction in the *masking* element or dynamic EQ only while needed. Do not carve a fixed universal “voice band” or boost all SFX highs for audibility.
5. **Use restrained automation.** If dialogue needs support, compare a manually shaped **1–3 dB** reduction of the relevant music/SFX bus as a starting experiment, not a required duck. Tune entry/recovery to speech and phrasing; reject audible breathing or music that repeatedly caves in. Prefer element-specific control when available.
6. **Preserve microdynamics.** Clip-gain outliers before bus compression. Compare processors bypassed at matched loudness. Avoid mastering every cue to the same peak: small details and hero gestures should not become equally insistent.
7. **Separate stems.** Retain music, any voice, material contacts, digital accents, travel/texture, and shared reverb as independently controllable stems. Check bus summation, not only isolated assets.

### Translation listening

- **Laptop speakers:** does the tactile body still read without low bass? Does the clean digital component turn into an irritating pin? Solve missing body with useful midrange structure, not indiscriminate loudness.
- **Phone speaker:** are semantic events distinguishable at a modest playback level? Is a sustained tone exposed or nasal? Is voice, if any, effortless? Do not expect sub-bass or stereo width to carry meaning.
- **Headphones:** check transient discomfort, hiss, distracting pan jumps, resonant ringing, and tiny artifacts that speakers hide. Use comfortable levels; never compensate for a weak design by extreme playback volume.
- **Mono fold-down:** check lost accents, cancellations, shifted prominence, and reverb masking. Important information must not depend on anti-phase width.
- **Low-level full mix:** ask whether the intended foreground is still the foreground. This is a listening test, not proof that the mix will work on every device or in noise.

## 8. Iterate on a representative 5–10 seconds before the full film

**Proposed pilot: 37.5–45.5 seconds (8 seconds at plan defaults).** It spans the end of Cost, the 39–40.2-second entry slide, and the first part of native Flow. It tests a hold, continuous motion, a new chapter, and early information reveal. Confirm this passage in the current picture before committing; revise the window if retiming removes those events.

Keep picture, music excerpt, cue positions, room, and perceived playback level constant:

- **A — tactile lead:** rounded material bodies; minimal digital articulation.
- **B — digital edge:** same event selection and envelope lengths, slightly more of the digital articulation layer. Do not also raise level or add more cues.
- **C — subtractive:** use the preferred A/B timbre but remove the least essential accents. This separately tests density.

Include music-only as a baseline where feasible. Compare in a blinded or neutral-label order and level-match by measurement **and** listening; identical peak normalization is not sufficient. Do not mix timbre, timing, density, and loudness changes into one undocumented alternative.

Ask the user concrete questions:
1. “What moment drew your attention first? Was that the right story point?”
2. “Does the slide feel soft but controlled, or airy/generic, mechanical, or heavy?”
3. “Which exact sound, if any, feels like a phone notification, toy, stock click, or alarm?”
4. “Where did an onset feel early, late, or disconnected from the movement?”
5. “Did you miss a musical detail or spoken word because of the effects?”
6. “Which sounds could disappear without losing anything? Where did silence feel intentional versus unfinished?”
7. “Would you still prefer this version at the same volume on a phone and after three repeats?”

Log answers by timestamp and proposed change. Obtain approval of the palette and density **before** scoring all 62 seconds. Then test a second short diagnostic passage covering warning/cluster behavior, followed by the ending; the first pilot does not validate those families. Finally review the complete film for cumulative fatigue, arc, and cross-chapter consistency.

## 9. Technical validation: necessary, not an artistic verdict

No measurements below have been performed on the supplied recording or a future mix.

- **Integrity:** verify the intended export duration, channel layout, sample rate, start offset, and audio/video sync. A proposed working handoff is 48 kHz / 24-bit PCM stems plus the delivery encode, unless the actual pipeline requires something else. Preserve original synthesis patch/seed data.
- **Clipping:** inspect each synthesis layer, buses, and output for clipping, unstable resonances, invalid samples, DC, abrupt edits, and discontinuities. A safe master output does not undo distortion introduced upstream.
- **True peak:** measure dBTP, not only dBFS sample peaks. Reconstructed/encoded audio can exceed stored sample peaks. [S11] For an audition export, a provisional ceiling such as **−1 dBTP** is an experiment in headroom, **not** a universal final specification; choose the delivery requirement and recheck the decoded lossy file. Reduce level further if the actual encode overshoots or sounds damaged.
- **Loudness:** reset the meter and measure **integrated LUFS over the exact full film**; also log **short-term loudness** around reveals/transitions and note momentary spikes. Record meter/standard/version, true-peak maximum, and any gain reduction. A looped pilot’s integrated value is not the full film’s value. Loudness range on a short, sparse film is descriptive, not a pass/fail aesthetic score. [S10]
- **No universal loudness target:** identify the publishing platform/client specification and whether it normalizes playback before setting final loudness. FabFilter’s example delivery targets are not proof of current policies for every platform. Do not blindly master this film to “−14 LUFS because internet,” nor to a broadcast target without a broadcast requirement.
- **Translation:** perform actual mono, laptop, phone, headphones, low-level, and delivery-encode listening. Check plugin latency compensation and cue alignment in the export, not just the editor preview.
- **Tail boundaries:** fade/resolve within the approved ending; seek/preview behavior must not conceal clipped tails at export. No silent change to the plan’s 62-second default.

**Meters can demonstrate headroom and consistency; they cannot demonstrate professional taste, emotional fit, intelligibility in all contexts, or user approval.**

## 10. Legal provenance and original-work discipline

This is a production checklist, not legal advice; no particular library asset or license has been cleared here.

- Prefer original oscillator/noise/modal patches for these abstract families. Save author, creation date, patch/code version, parameter set, seed, renderer/tool version, and exported-file hash. Confirm whether any preset actually embeds third-party samples or wavetables.
- For licensed assets, record provider, asset ID/title, creator, source URL, acquisition date, license text/version, purchase receipt where applicable, attribution requirement, permitted media/territory/term, and restrictions. Verify synchronization in a commercial/promotional film, modification, client handoff, and advertising/social distribution. A “royalty-free” label alone is insufficient.
- Distinguish embedding in a finished film from redistributing raw samples or stems. Check that any repository or client delivery does not expose source files beyond the license. A commissioned designer agreement should cover the needed rights and stem delivery.
- Check convolution IRs, presets, sample instruments, and any training/source-material restrictions of tools actually used—not only obvious downloaded WAVs.
- Do not extract sounds from Antfood’s films, app notification packs, or streaming demos. Public access is not a reuse license. Learn from process and function, not a copied sonic logo.
- Record all transformations and layered dependencies. An original top layer does not make an unlicensed underlying sample original.
- Audit the final replacement manifest against every old SFX dependency, including baked-in music effects. Preserve earlier experiments and their files unchanged.

## 11. Listening acceptance rubric

**Proposed human rubric**, to be completed after audition. Rate each dimension **1–5**: 1 = clearly wrong; 3 = workable but distracting/inconsistent; 5 = intentional and convincing. A sensible pilot gate is **at least 4 in every dimension**, with no hard failures below. This is a project decision aid, not a scientifically validated taste metric or a score supplied by this research.

| Dimension | Observable acceptance question | Reject / revise when |
|---|---|---|
| Narrative hierarchy | Can the listener name the intended foreground moment without being prompted? | Decorative cues repeatedly steal attention. |
| Material identity | Do unrelated chapters feel made from the same small palette while retaining distinct roles? | Random stock clicks, glass pings, slams, or whooshes suggest different brands. |
| Softness + precision | Are attacks clear without pinching, and bodies tactile without flab? | Harsh ticks, nasal ringing, cartoon boings, or smeared arrivals. |
| Sync and phrasing | Do contact accents land and anticipations feel motivated? | A sound appears to cause an event too early, arrives late, or ignores the easing. |
| Restraint / fatigue | Is the passage still pleasant after three repeats? Can the quiet moments be explained? | Constant typing, particle chatter, warning anxiety, or effect saturation. |
| Music / voice relationship | Are melody, rhythm, and every necessary spoken word easy to follow? | SFX win only by being louder; music pumps or voice becomes effortful. |
| Translation | Does the foreground order survive phone, laptop, headphones, and mono? | A key cue disappears or becomes painful on a common device. |
| Arc and ending | Does the full film develop and release, with the static logo feeling complete? | Every chapter restarts, all moments are climaxes, or the tail is cut off. |

**Hard release gates:** no uncleared assets or inherited old SFX; no unintended clipping/clicks; no semantic failure in mono; no unintelligible required speech; no painful warning/transient; no export/timing mismatch; no unapproved visual or duration changes. Log the actual tester, device, playback context, chosen alternative, measurements, and remaining concerns. A missing listening test is “not tested,” not a pass.
